Dear Board Members,

I am writing to formally request authorization and funding for the **College Readiness Initiative**, a district-wide analytics program designed to reverse the decline in postsecondary enrollment and strengthen our capacity to identify and support at-risk students before the intervention window closes.

### The Problem

Over the past three years, Lakeview Unified's college enrollment rate has declined by **eight (8) percentage points**. This decline is not isolated to a single school, feeder pattern, or demographic subgroup — it is systemic, cutting across our Public, Charter, and Private school populations. While national enrollment trends have softened in the same period, Lakeview's decline exceeds state and regional averages by a significant margin.

The consequences are visible across the district. Guidance counselors report increasing numbers of seniors who have not completed a single college application. AP course enrollment has plateaued despite expanded offerings. And our own internal data shows that students identified as "at risk" in their junior year rarely recover to college-ready status — by that point, the academic, financial, and psychological barriers have compounded beyond what a single semester of intervention can address.

**We are identifying struggling students too late.**

### The Proposal

The College Readiness Initiative centers on the development of a **predictive early-warning system** that will identify students at risk of low academic performance and poor college readiness — beginning no later than the end of their sophomore year.

The system will be built on Lakeview's Student Information System (SIS) database, which currently contains approximately **300,000 student records** spanning thirty months of enrollment data. These records include 22 data fields covering:

- **Academic metrics:** GPA, SAT scores, AP course completion, attendance rate
- **Study habits:** Weekly study hours, tutoring utilization, library visits, online resource usage
- **School environment:** School type (Public/Charter/Private), class size
- **Family background:** Family income, parent education level
- **Well-being indicators:** Extracurricular involvement, discipline incidents, sleep hours, stress level

The initiative will produce two validated predictive models:

1. **Academic Performance Classification** — A three-tier system (High / Medium / Low) that flags students for appropriate levels of support and monitoring at the start of each semester.

2. **College Readiness Score Prediction** — A continuous index estimating each student's preparedness for postsecondary success, enabling targeted resource allocation for students "on the bubble."

### Budget

### Data Quality Considerations

I want to be transparent with the Board about a known challenge. The SIS migration completed the previous summer introduced data quality issues into the student record database. The analytics team, led by Dr. Carmen Reyes, has identified missing values in approximately 3% of records, suspected data-entry errors in roughly 5% of records, and a small number of statistical outliers that require investigation.

Dr. Reyes assures me that these issues are manageable and consistent with what any large district experiences during a system migration. A thorough data quality audit will be the first phase of the initiative, and the team's cleaning methodology will be fully documented and available for Board review.

### Equity Commitment

I have asked Director Diane Marchetti and the District Equity Council to conduct a parallel review of the early-warning system at every stage of development. The Council will evaluate the system for potential bias, disparate impact, and unintended consequences — including the risk that predictive labels may influence teacher expectations. No student-facing tool will be deployed without Equity Council approval.

This is not a formality. The research literature is clear that predictive systems in education carry risks alongside their benefits. We must build a system that identifies students who need support without defining them by their disadvantage.

### Recommendation

I recommend that the Board authorize the College Readiness Initiative and approve the $2.4 million budget allocation. The eight-point enrollment decline is not a problem that will self-correct. Our students — particularly those from families without a college-going tradition — need earlier identification and more sustained support. This initiative gives us the tools to provide both.

I am available to discuss this proposal in detail at the Board's convenience.
