# Exhibit A — Clean Buildings Act: Legislative Summary and Utility Compliance Framework

**STATE ENERGY OFFICE — PUBLIC NOTICE**

---

## Official Summary of the Clean Buildings Act

**Enacted:** Current legislative session
**Effective Date:** Immediately upon signature
**Administering Agencies:** State Energy Office; Public Utilities Commission
**Applicable Entities:** All regulated utilities serving 100,000 or more commercial and residential accounts

---

### Section 1: Legislative Intent

The legislature finds that building energy consumption accounts for approximately 40% of total greenhouse gas emissions within the state. Aging building stock, inefficient HVAC systems, inadequate insulation, and deferred maintenance represent the single largest opportunity for measurable emissions reduction without displacing economic activity.

The legislature further finds that existing voluntary efficiency programs, while well-intentioned, have achieved adoption rates below 15% across the building portfolio, with participation concentrated among newer commercial buildings whose owners have the resources and incentive to invest in upgrades. Low-income residential buildings, older institutional facilities, and small commercial properties have been systematically underserved by voluntary programs.

This Act establishes a mandatory framework to achieve measurable, verifiable reductions in building energy consumption across all building types and all communities served by regulated utilities.

---

### Section 2: Mandatory Reduction Target

**§ 2.1** Each regulated utility shall achieve a **30% reduction in aggregate building energy consumption** across its service territory within **five (5) years** of the effective date of this Act.

**§ 2.2** The reduction shall be measured against a baseline established using the utility's most recent 12 months of building energy consumption data, as reported to the Public Utilities Commission.

**§ 2.3** Utilities shall demonstrate **measurable annual progress** toward the 30% target, with interim benchmarks as follows:

| Year | Cumulative Reduction Target |
|------|---------------------------|
| 1 | 5% |
| 2 | 12% |
| 3 | 20% |
| 4 | 26% |
| 5 | 30% |

**§ 2.4** Failure to meet annual interim benchmarks shall trigger a compliance review by the Public Utilities Commission, with authority to levy financial penalties of up to $500,000 per percentage point of shortfall.

---

### Section 3: Retrofit Investment Fund

**§ 3.1** The Act establishes the **Building Retrofit Investment Fund**, capitalized through a combination of state appropriations, utility contributions, and federal efficiency matching grants.

**§ 3.2** Fund allocation by utility service territory:

| Utility | Service Territory | Buildings Served | Fund Allocation |
|---------|------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| GreenGrid Utilities | Metro Region A | ~350,000 | $50,000,000 |
| Northern Power Cooperative | Rural Region B | ~120,000 | $18,000,000 |
| Coastal Energy Partners | Region C | ~210,000 | $32,000,000 |
| **Total** | **Statewide** | **~680,000** | **$100,000,000** |

**§ 3.3** Fund expenditures shall be directed toward **building retrofit subsidies**, including but not limited to:
- HVAC system replacement or upgrade
- Insulation improvement
- Window replacement or thermal coating
- Solar panel installation
- Smart meter deployment and energy management systems
- Building envelope sealing and weatherization

**§ 3.4** Utilities shall develop a **data-driven targeting methodology** to identify buildings where retrofit investment will yield the greatest measurable energy savings. The methodology shall be submitted to the State Energy Office for review within **90 days** of the effective date of this Act.

---

### Section 4: Data and Reporting Requirements

**§ 4.1** Each utility shall maintain a comprehensive **Building Energy Database** containing, at minimum:
- Building physical characteristics (type, age, floor area, occupancy)
- Equipment and systems data (HVAC, insulation, windows, solar capacity)
- Energy consumption records (monthly or quarterly readings)
- Weather conditions at the time of each reading
- Efficiency classification for each building

**§ 4.2** Utilities shall submit **quarterly progress reports** to the Public Utilities Commission, including:
- Number of buildings retrofitted
- Total fund expenditure to date
- Measured energy savings per building and in aggregate
- Distribution of retrofit investments by building type and geographic area
- Data quality assessment and any corrections to baseline measurements

**§ 4.3** All targeting methodologies, predictive models, and allocation algorithms shall be documented and made available for public comment prior to deployment.

---

### Section 5: Equity Provisions

**§ 5.1** The legislature recognizes that energy efficiency programs have historically underserved low-income communities and communities of color. The Clean Buildings Act includes the following equity provisions:

**§ 5.2** No less than **25%** of each utility's Retrofit Investment Fund allocation shall be directed toward buildings in **designated environmental justice communities**, as defined by the State Environmental Justice Mapping Tool.

**§ 5.3** Utilities shall report the demographic distribution of retrofit investments annually, disaggregated by building type, geographic area, and community income level.

**§ 5.4** The Environmental Justice Advisory Council, appointed by the Governor, shall review utility targeting methodologies and issue public recommendations regarding equitable fund distribution.

**§ 5.5** Nothing in this section shall be construed to reduce the aggregate 30% reduction mandate. Utilities are expected to achieve both efficiency and equity objectives.

---

### Section 6: Accountability and Enforcement

**§ 6.1** The Public Utilities Commission shall conduct **annual compliance reviews** of each participating utility.

**§ 6.2** Utilities that fail to meet annual interim benchmarks (§ 2.3) shall be required to submit a **corrective action plan** within 30 days.

**§ 6.3** Persistent non-compliance — defined as missing interim benchmarks in two or more consecutive years — may result in:
- Financial penalties up to $500,000 per percentage point of shortfall
- Mandatory reallocation of unspent fund balances to the State Energy Office for direct administration
- Public disclosure of non-compliance in the utility's annual report to ratepayers

**§ 6.4** Utilities that exceed annual benchmarks may carry forward excess reductions to offset future shortfalls.

### Section 7: GreenGrid Utilities — Compliance Obligations

As the largest regulated utility in the state by building count, GreenGrid Utilities bears the following specific obligations:

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| Service territory | ~350,000 buildings |
| Fund allocation | $50,000,000 |
| Baseline year | Most recent 12-month consumption period |
| Targeting methodology submission | Within 90 days of effective date |
| First annual progress report | Within 12 months of effective date |
| Environmental justice allocation floor | 25% of fund ($12,500,000 minimum) |
| Quarterly reporting to PUC | Begins immediately |

**Key Challenge:** GreenGrid's building portfolio spans four sectors — Residential, Commercial, Industrial, and Institutional — with widely varying energy profiles, equipment conditions, and retrofit potential. The utility must develop a targeting model capable of identifying the buildings where $50 million in retrofit investment will achieve the greatest measurable impact, while meeting the 25% environmental justice floor and maintaining public trust in the allocation process.

---

### Implications for GreenGrid Analytics Team

The Act's combination of an aggressive reduction target (30%), a fixed funding envelope ($50M), an equity mandate (25% EJ floor), and public transparency requirements (methodology disclosure, demographic reporting) creates a constrained optimization problem with no precedent in the state's regulatory history.

The targeting methodology will be scrutinized by the Public Utilities Commission, the State Energy Office, the Environmental Justice Advisory Council, and the public. Every allocation decision must be defensible — analytically, ethically, and politically.
